One box between your cameras and everything you wish they could do.

Nexovia is not a camera company. It is the intelligence layer that sits on the network you already built. This page walks the whole path — how a frame gets in, what happens to it, and how it reaches you.

01
Ingest

It finds your cameras before you finish reading the quickstart.

Plug the Hive into a free port on your switch and power it. On boot it sweeps the local subnet for anything answering ONVIF discovery or exposing an RTSP endpoint, and presents you a list. You tick the cameras you want it to watch. Credentials go in once, stay encrypted at rest on the device, and are never transmitted anywhere else.

  • ONVIF WS-Discovery plus manual RTSP URL entry for cameras that hide
  • H.264 and H.265, main or sub-stream, per camera
  • Reads a passive copy — your NVR keeps its own recording untouched
  • Reconnect and back-off logic for cameras that drop overnight
  • Per-camera frame-rate throttling so one 4K feed cannot starve the rest
02
Inference

The part everyone else does in someone else's data centre.

Decoded frames go straight to the GPU on the Hive. Detection, pose estimation, tracking and re-identification all run locally, on hardware sitting in your building. Nothing about understanding your footage requires a round trip to a cloud, which is why the budget stays at 23ms instead of whatever your upload speed happens to be that day.

  • Object detection for people, vehicles and the objects that matter per site
  • Pose keypoints — the difference between "lying down" and "fell down"
  • Multi-object tracking with IDs that survive brief occlusion
  • Re-identification to carry a subject between overlapping cameras
  • Models are swappable per camera, so a stockroom lens and an aisle lens differ
See every detection class
03
Event engine

A detection is not an alert.
This is the layer that decides.

A raw model will happily tell you there is a person in frame nine hundred times an hour. The event engine is what turns that firehose into the four things that actually needed you today — through zones, schedules, dwell thresholds and deduplication.

Zones

Draw polygons on the frame. Behind the counter, the stockroom door, the loading bay. The same person is unremarkable in one and an event in another.

Per-camera geometry

Schedules

Store hours are one ruleset, overnight is another. Presence in the yard at 2pm is a delivery. Presence at 2am is the whole reason you bought this.

Time-aware rules

Dwell & sequence

Loitering is time. Theft is a sequence. Rules can require a state to hold for N seconds, or require two detections to occur in order before anything fires.

Stateful, not single-frame

Deduplication

One incident produces one alert, not forty. Events collapse across frames and across cameras that saw the same thing from different angles.

Alert fatigue is the enemy

Clip sealing

When an event fires, the Hive writes a clip covering the seconds before and after, hashes it, and stores it against an evidence ID so the record is defensible later.

Pre-roll + post-roll

Escalation chain

Who gets told, in what order, and what happens if nobody acknowledges. Push, on-site strobe or speaker, webhook into whatever you already run.

Configurable per event class
04
Console & app

The answer arrives before you go looking for it.

Most security software assumes someone is sitting in front of it. Nexovia assumes nobody is. The alert finds the person on call, on their phone, with the clip already attached and the event already named — so the first decision happens in seconds, not after a drive back to the office.

  • Push notification carrying the sealed clip, not just a timestamp to go find
  • Live view of any camera on the site, from anywhere
  • Acknowledge, resolve or escalate in a single tap, with an audit trail
  • Device health: uptime, temperature, CPU, memory, per-camera online state
  • Multi-site switching for operators who run more than one location
Search

Describe it. Don't scrub for it.

Every detection the Hive makes is indexed with what it was, where it was and when. That turns the worst job in security — finding one moment inside a week of footage — into a sentence. "Red hoodie, aisle three, after four." The console returns the candidate clips instead of a timeline you have to drag through.

  • Filter by class, camera, zone and time window together
  • Jump straight to the sealed clip rather than a raw recording offset
  • Export a single incident, or a stitched cross-camera timeline for one subject
Data & security posture

Where your footage goes, in plain language.

Video storage
Stays on your site. Continuous recording remains with your existing NVR. The Hive retains short sealed clips tied to events, on local storage.
ON-PREM
Inference
On-device, always. Frames are analysed by the GPU inside the Hive. Raw video is not streamed out to a third party in order to be understood.
EDGE
Cloud role
Account, site and device records, plus event metadata for the console and app. Designed so the detection path keeps working when the link is down.
METADATA
Remote access
Outbound tunnel only. The Hive dials out. No inbound port forwarding, no exposing a camera or an NVR to the public internet.
NO OPEN PORTS
Isolation
Every site is scoped to its own devices and cameras. Operators only see the locations their account is a member of.
PER-SITE
Offline behaviour
Detection, rules and local clip sealing continue without internet. Events queue and sync when the link returns.
DEGRADES GRACEFULLY
Deployment

What going live actually looks like.

Before anything ships

We look at your camera list

Make, model, count, resolution and where each one points. That tells us the stream count, which Hive you need, and honestly whether any angle is too poor to be worth promising anything about.

Minute 0 — 10

You plug it in

One free port on your existing switch and a power outlet. No re-cabling, no camera work, no touching the NVR, and no specialist required — if you can plug in a router you can do this. It boots and dials out to us.

Minute 10 — 45

We configure it remotely

Screen share, your cameras on our side of the tunnel: we draw the zones that matter, set the hours, pick the detection classes per camera, and agree the escalation order for each event type. No engineer on site.

First week

You settle the thresholds

Every site has its own false-positive personality — a reflective floor, a swinging door, a delivery route that looks like an intrusion. Every dwell time, zone and confidence threshold is editable in the console, and support is there if you want a second opinion on a setting.

Ongoing

Model updates without a truck roll

Detection models and firmware update over the tunnel. New classes become available to sites already running without anyone coming back out.

Anything this page didn't answer?

Subnet layout, VLANs, an existing VMS you need it to sit beside — the awkward integration questions are the ones we would rather hear before you buy, not after.
